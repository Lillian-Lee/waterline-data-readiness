What changed between the two data versions?

This example compares fictional records from 30 June 2025 and 31 October 2025. It shows how to check changes in asset and capital-project records, record why they matter and ask the right role to confirm them. It uses no Waitī Waters or council records.

What the example shows

Compare project amounts only when they refer to the same project, financial year, scope of work and type of estimate, and when the currency and unit are confirmed. One fictional finance owner confirms that an amount is in thousands of New Zealand dollars. That means 7 of 12 projects have comparable estimates, up from 6.

The NZD 25,000 difference is between estimates, not actual spending. For five projects, the two estimates still cannot be compared fairly. The original data file is unchanged.

Why this matters to Waitī Waters

In its public October 2026 update, Waitī Waters said it was requesting council data files to assess how much cleaning and migration work was needed. It also described developing measures for internal performance and external reporting.

This fictional exercise does not use those files. It shows a related step: check whether records and amounts can be compared, record what needs confirmation, and keep unclear figures out of the report until someone checks them.

The region names, records, dates, amounts and review roles are fictional. The suggested roles are examples, not actual Waitī Waters responsibilities.

How the comparisons are made

  • Match records using both the region and its local ID. Different regions may reuse the same ID.
  • Compare amounts only when the project, financial year, work scope, estimate type, currency and unit match.
  • If information is missing or unclear, leave the amount out until a data owner confirms it.
  • Do not connect a project to a pipe without a verified link. Keep this fictional data separate from the public records.
